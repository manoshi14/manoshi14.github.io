Researcher & Writer · Natural Language Processing

I am a researcher in natural language processing, happiest when a hard question about language refuses to give up its answer easily. I care about making language technology trustworthy, fair and inclusive, especially for low-resource languages that still sit at the edges of the field. Three questions keep pulling me back: can we trust what a model says it is doing? Does every language and community have a place in these systems? Does AI in the classroom truly help students learn? I chase them through interpretable models, fairness in large language models and human-centered evaluation.

I completed my BSc in Computer Science and Engineering at Ahsanullah University of Science and Technology in June 2026, and I am now a research assistant there with Md. Rakibul Islam, studying why sentiment and sarcasm models falter when language shifts. Alongside the lab, I intern in the AML/CFT team at bKash, Bangladesh's leading fintech company.

Writing is the other half of how I think. My articles in The Daily Observer and The New Nation follow two threads: the quiet inner life, in thought, gratitude and the small things we overlook, and what AI is changing about how Bangladesh learns. For me, research and writing are one habit: paying close attention to meaning.

Human-Supervised Concept Bottlenecks for Interpretable Bangla Text Classification

Farzana Zahid Manoshi,

We collect 436,966 dense human instance–concept judgments for Bangla sarcasm and show that human-supervised concept bottleneck models beat similarity-supervised ones on all nine encoders. GPT-5.x models reach about 79% label accuracy yet agree only modestly with humans on concepts.

Beyond Stance Detection: Can Cause–Solution Annotation for Bengali Climate News Transfer Zero-Shot to Hindi?

Farzana Zahid Manoshi,

We extend a Bengali climate news corpus with a 16-category, Bangladesh-specific cause taxonomy paired with source-grounded solutions, and release the first Hindi dataset of its kind (2,000 articles). A Bangla-trained IndicBERTv2 classifier (F1-macro 0.730) falls to 0.396 under zero-shot transfer to Hindi, mostly because of precision and calibration failures.

Research

I am drawn to a simple idea: a language model should be right for reasons people can understand, and it should work for everyone who depends on it. My research sits where interpretability, fairness and language diversity meet, and it aims to make NLP systems more transparent, more equitable and more useful in the real world.

Research experience

Research Assistant · Ahsanullah University of Science and Technology

I work with Md. Rakibul Islam of the Department of CSE. I investigate which language-variety shifts (idioms, spelling, code-switching) make sentiment and sarcasm models unstable, using human-validated counterfactual pairs that change one linguistic feature at a time.

Research interests

  • Natural language processing for low-resource and multilingual settings
  • Artificial intelligence: interpretable, concept-based and trustworthy models
  • Fairness, bias and responsible deployment of large language models
  • AI in education: evidence of learning impact from LLMs in the classroom
  • Human-aligned evaluation and large-scale annotation
  • Computational pragmatics: intent, sarcasm and misinformation in language
  • NLP for climate and society across South Asian languages
  • Meta-research: systematic audits of how the NLP field evaluates itself

Research directions

Interpretable and trustworthy language models

I want language models whose decisions can be inspected, questioned and corrected by the people who use them. I am interested in concept-based explanations, faithfulness, and human-in-the-loop supervision, and in measuring when a model's reasons truly match human understanding.

Language equity and low-resource NLP

Progress in NLP is still concentrated in a few languages. I work toward inclusive language technology: building datasets, benchmarks and evaluation practices that bring under-represented languages and communities into the field on their own terms.

Fairness and responsible use of LLMs

I study how bias, uneven performance and weak evaluation affect people when LLMs are deployed, and how to design auditing methods that hold systems accountable before they reach the public.

AI for education and learning

I like looking for the gaps and loopholes in a field. In educational NLP I ask whether LLMs used in classrooms genuinely improve learning, whether their use is fair to all students, and what rigorous evidence of impact should look like.

Language, intent and misinformation

Meaning often lives beyond the words: in irony, implication and persuasion. I am interested in computational pragmatics and in how language is used to mislead, and in building systems that can reason about intent.
